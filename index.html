Protein — in a calorie deficit with hard training, higher protein did better in one trial. Aim for 170–190 g a day (about 1.8–2.0 g per kg); 150 g is the floor. The meals below sit near the top of that range.
Calories are an estimate — 2,250 is a calculated starting point, not a measurement. Judge it by your 4-week trend in weight and waist, and recheck every 4–6 weeks as you lose weight, because maintenance calories drop too.

Eating with family

When it's a shared meal, not your own plate:

Build your plate — protein first (fill a third of the plate), a modest portion of rice, then load up on any vegetables or salad on the table. Go back for more protein/veg before more carbs if still hungry.
Wheat can hide in shared dishes — watch for: bread served alongside the meal, thickened stews or gravies (wheat flour is a common thickener), battered or breaded meats, and pasta-based sides. When unsure, ask or stick to the plainly grilled/roasted items and rice.

Meal timing around workouts

Before — a balanced meal 1.5–2 hrs pre-training. Training soon after waking? Keep it light: a banana or a couple of eggs.
After — timing matters less than your total protein for the day. Eat when it suits you. If you train hungry and feel weak, have something light first.

Sleep & recovery

Sleep — you're already at 8+ hrs. Keep it exactly as is, and try to keep the same sleep/wake time daily.
Rest day — Day 7 is a full rest day. Respect it, don't fill it with extra training.
Sauna & jacuzzi — great for recovery, not for fat loss. Sweat lost there is water, back within hours.

Warm-up & injury prevention

Weeks 1–2 — technique only. Light weight, perfect form. Add load from week 3 onward.
Muscle soreness (DOMS) — expect real soreness in the first weeks. It usually eases over 2–3 weeks as your body adapts (the second time you do an exercise, you get less sore). Joint pain is different — stop a set immediately if you feel that.
Pain vs. burn — muscle burn during a set is normal. Joint pain is not — stop the set if you feel it.
Progression — open any exercise to see what you did last time, and try to beat it. When you reach the top of the rep range on every set, add a little weight next time. Never a sudden jump.
Warm-up — structured warm-ups (squats, lunges, balance) cut injuries in team-sport trials, and it's quick. I found no trials in gym lifters. Add 1–2 lighter sets of your first lift too.
Deload only if you need one — there's no tested schedule for beginners. If your lifts stall or you feel run down for about two weeks, take one lighter week: same exercises, roughly a third fewer sets and 20–30% less weight.

Hands & grip

Grip — farmer's carries on Days 1 and 5 train your hands, and so does all your pulling. If your hands let go before your back or legs are tired on a lift, note it in your log.
Straps — only on heavy Romanian deadlift sets, when your grip fails before your hamstrings do. Skip them elsewhere so your grip still gets trained.
See a doctor if — you get numbness, tingling or pain in your hands, you drop things, or the weakness came on suddenly.

Limits of this evidence

Most of these trials had 10–70 people and ran 6–12 weeks, in young men, women or older adults. Few were in someone like you, a beginner carrying extra fat, so treat this as the best available guidance, not a guarantee.
Not checked yet — how accurate the calorie estimate is, machines versus free weights for beginners, and cardio and fat loss. I read abstracts, not full papers, and my PubMed searches were not a formal systematic review.
